# Table 7: the long and the short book, and the section 3.7 claim

_Generated by `scripts/claims/table7.py`; regenerate with `python scripts/claims/table7.py --report report/replication/claims/TABLE7_REGRESSIONS.md --csv report/replication/claims/table7_regressions.csv`._

## 1. What the paper says

Section 3.7, “Risk and return of the long and short positions” (pp. 819-820):

> The table demonstrates that much of the pairs risk-adjusted excess
> return comes from the short portfolio, which contains the stocks that
> have increased in value relative to their counterparts prior to opening of
> the pair. By contrast, the alphas of the long portfolio containing the
> stocks that decreased in value relative to their counterparts are smaller,
> and insignificantly different from zero for the top 5 and top 20 portfolios.
> The asymmetry of the results provides further evidence that the returns to
> pairs are not due to simple one-month mean reversion. And because
> much of the abnormal return comes from the short position, which
> experienced an increase in relative value prior to opening, it is unlikely
> that the returns are driven by a reward for unrealized bankruptcy risk.
> The robustness of our results to the cost of short sales will be discussed in
> Section 3.9.
>

The table note (p. 821) fixes the estimator:

> Monthly risk profile for the long and short positions of the pairs portfolios formed and traded according to the ‘‘wait one day’’ rule discussed in the text. The returns in the
> bottom half of the table are in excess of the 30-day Treasury bill returns. The risk adjustment includes the Fama-French factors, as well as Momentum and the Reversal factors
> discussed in the text. The t-statistics are computed using Newey-West standard errors with six lags. Absolute kurtosis is reported.
>

Table 7 prints, for each of four portfolios, two columns of seventeen rows: four of portfolio performance (mean, Newey-West standard error, standard deviation, monthly serial correlation) and thirteen of a five-factor regression (intercept and its t-statistic, five loadings with theirs, R2), once for the long book and once for the short.

The paper's own arithmetic fixes the sign. Its Table 4 intercept equals the difference of Table 7's two intercepts to within one unit of the last digit - 0.00545 = 0.00103 + 0.00442, 0.00764 = 0.00243 + 0.00521, 0.00714 against 0.00287 + 0.00426 = 0.00713, 0.00512 = -0.00101 + 0.00613 (pp. 814, 821) - so the pair is long book minus short book, the Short column is the shorted stocks' return, and the alpha the paper gives the short portfolio is the negative of the printed number. The tables below keep the paper's sign; the claim is stated on the contribution.

## 2. Method

Every number comes from the shipped cohort cache. `build_cohort` already writes each portfolio's long and short book as their own monthly return series (`long_book` / `short_book`, from `project.evaluation.portfolio_legs`, equation 2 applied to each leg) beside the portfolio's own fully-invested series.

`scripts/claims/table7.py` overlap-averages those books exactly as the driver overlap-averages the portfolio (Jegadeesh-Titman: equal-weighted mean over the cohorts whose six-month window covers the month), subtracts French's one-month T-bill from each book, and runs `factor_regression` / `factor_tstats` - OLS with Newey-West standard errors at six lags - on Table 4's five factors: Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, UMD and the reversal factor. No cohort is rebuilt and no cache is written.

Run: 474 cohorts, 474 months, cache `data/processed/replication/vol_common/cohorts`, meta `{'normalization': 'continued', 'format': 5, 'exclude_same_issuer': True, 'max_stale_share': 0.95, 'telephone_sic': '4811', 'trigger_sigma': 'rms', 'short_weights': 'position'}`.

Factors: `data/raw/public/french` (the pulled copies; `data/raw/french` in a checkout without `git-lfs` holds pointers only).

### Ambiguities, and the reading chosen

1. **The reversal factor.** The paper builds its own and never publishes it. Primary: French's ST_Rev, the factor the scored Tables 4 and 8 use. Alternative: the losers-only long book that REVIEW_NOTES section 51 identifies as the paper's factor's fingerprint. Both reported; ST_Rev is primary because it is the construction the paper describes.
2. **Excess or raw.** The note puts the bottom half in excess of the 30-day bill, so the regression's dependent variable is the book less French's RF; the four performance rows are raw.
3. **Which compounding.** The paper compounds each leg to a monthly return and subtracts; our shipped portfolio series compounds the daily difference instead. The books do not depend on the choice - only the pair mean does, by 1-3 bp (largest gap here 3.2 bp).
4. **Overlap weighting.** Equal weight over the cohorts covering the month, the driver's convention, so Table 7 and our Table 1/Table 8 are like for like.
5. **Significance.** “Insignificantly different from zero” read as |t| < 1.96 (two-sided 5%); “much of the ... return comes from the short portfolio” read as a short-book contribution of more than half the pair alpha.
6. **T-statistics.** Newey-West, six lags, no small-sample correction, as in the scored Tables 4 and 8 (REVIEW_NOTES section 50).

## 5. The effect against our own baseline

Our levels sit above the paper's for reasons already documented (the era split in the stock universe, OPEN_QUESTIONS Q8, and the return tails of REVIEW_NOTES section 52), so the claim is tested on our decomposition beside the paper's, not on the levels. The pair alpha is the control regression's intercept - the shipped Table 4 alpha - and the books' are Table 7's. A book's share is its alpha over the pair alpha, so the two shares exceed one by the legs' covariance term.

## 6. The claim test

| test | ours, ST_Rev | ours, losers-only | paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| short contribution exceeds the long alpha, top 5 / top 20 | yes | no | yes |
| long alpha insignificant, abs(t) below 1.96, top 5 / top 20 | no | no | yes |
| short alpha significant, t < -1.96, top 5 / top 20 | yes | yes | yes |
| short book carries over half the books' combined alpha, top 5 / top 20 | yes | no | yes |

The shares in this section are of the two books' combined alpha, intercept(long) - intercept(short), which for the paper is its Table 4 intercept and for us differs from the portfolio's own alpha in section 5 by the compounding convention.
Short book's share of that alpha: top 5 63% and top 20 60% under ST_Rev (paper 81% and 68%); 52% and 49% under the losers-only factor.
Long-book alpha: top 5 0.0027 (t = 1.51), top 20 0.0036 (t = 2.17); the paper prints 0.00103 (0.47) and 0.00243 (1.27).

## 7. Verdict

**Not replicated as stated; the asymmetry replicates in substance for the top 5 and the top 20.**

- The short book carries most of the books' combined alpha for the two headline portfolios: 63% and 60% against the paper's 81% and 68%. Same sign, same side of one half, roughly four fifths of the paper's share: the paper's sign and order of magnitude.
- Its alpha is significant in every column: t = -2.78, -3.60, -3.24, -3.51 against the paper's -2.74, -3.35, -4.26, -5.22. All four are significant wherever the paper's are.
- The long book's alpha is smaller than the short book's for the top 5 and the top 20, as the paper says, and insignificant for the top 5 (t = 1.51 against the paper's 0.47).
- It is *not* insignificant for the top 20: t = 2.17 against the paper's 1.27, and 2.73 under the losers-only factor. The claim's own wording fails on one of its two named portfolios.
- For the two broad portfolios our attribution is inverted: the long book carries 63% of the pair alpha for the 20 after top 100 and 69% for all, where the paper's long book carries 40% and -20%.

Against the numeric standard the table fails: ST_Rev ✓ 39 / ~ 22 / ✗ 75 of 136, losers-only ✓ 36 / ~ 18 / ✗ 82 of 136. The binding constraint is the loadings, not the books: the books' means reproduce the paper's closely, while our market and SMB loadings differ and the intercept absorbs the difference - the Table 4 story, not a bug in this table.

## 8. Limitations

- The paper's reversal factor is unidentified (REVIEW_NOTES section 51). The two readings bracket the answer, and the top-20 long alpha is significant under both.
- The paper's cells are printed to five decimals but computed from a universe we do not reproduce exactly (era split, return tails), so a 10% band on an intercept of 0.1% a month is a tighter test than it looks.
- Cohorts are weighted equally across overlapping windows, as in our Table 1; the paper does not state its weighting.
- No confidence interval is put on the shares; the books' alphas are correlated through the same pairs, so their shares do not have a simple sampling distribution.
