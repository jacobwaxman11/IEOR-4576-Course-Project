**What would have to be true.** Table 7 prints a 24 bp long alpha for the top 20 (t 1.27), and the claim is its own sentence: “the alphas of the long portfolio containing the stocks that decreased in value relative to their counterparts are smaller, and insignificantly different from zero for the top 5 and top 20 portfolios” (p. 820). Our top-20 long book earns 36-50 bp over the full sample under every reading here (both vintages, both reversal series, both constructions, every lag count), and the readings that bring its |t| under 1.96 are window redefinitions of a full-sample table. To replicate, the long alpha would have to fall to about the paper's 24 bp; no sentence of the paper does that, so the verdict stays not replicated as stated.

**The asymmetry.** “much of the pairs risk-adjusted excess return comes from the short portfolio” (p. 820), read as the short book carrying more than half the pair alpha: the paper's Table 7 gives 81% and 68% for the top 5 and top 20. Ours, full sample and six lags: 50% and 47% on the scored series, 59% and 56% under ST_Rev. The asymmetry holds under ST_Rev but not on the scored series, and both series fall short of the paper's shares.
